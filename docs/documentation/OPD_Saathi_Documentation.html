1 Why OPD Saathi exists

Government outpatient departments (OPDs) see hundreds of patients every morning. Consultations last a few minutes, patients speak many languages and describe their problems in their own way, papers from earlier visits are scattered, and nobody in the waiting hall knows when their turn will come. The doctor spends much of those few minutes collecting basic history.

The problem statement: SIH26047

TitlePatient Case-Taking Software (SIH26047)
OrganisationMinistry of Ayush · All India Institute of Ayurveda (AIIA)
Category · themeSoftware · MedTech / BioTech / HealthTech
The needHistories are incomplete because consultations are short; AYUSH practice needs an even more extensive history (Prakriti, Vikriti, Agni); patients bring fragmented paper records; ABDM exists, but there is no patient-facing "first mile" tool that captures history and digitises documents before the visit.
Expected journeyIdentify and consent → converse → scan documents → summarise and route → physician reviews.

The wording was verified from two independent copies of the official entry (the official portal could not be fetched automatically). See docs/PROBLEM_STATEMENT.md.

What OPD Saathi deliberately does not do

Never
  • diagnose, name a likely disease, suggest medicines or doses, or give treatment advice;
  • show a patient their urgency score, or anyone else's position;
  • let AI lower urgency, or decide urgency on its own;
  • link two patient records automatically from a phone number or name.
Always
  • ask for consent before collecting anything;
  • let the patient hear and confirm their own history;
  • show staff why a patient is where they are in the queue;
  • keep working without internet and without AI (rules, tapping and typing).

3 The patient kiosk, step by step

A touch-screen kiosk: any tablet or PC with Chrome or Edge. Six steps are shown at the top of every screen: Agree → Details → Safety check → Questions → Review → Token. The patient can change language, go back, call staff or stop at any point.

3.1 Welcome and language

The patient taps हिन्दी, मराठी or English and Start patient check-in. The kiosk starts a server session (POST /intake/sessions), which creates a visit in the registering state and returns a two-hour patient token and the consent notice in that language.

Everything after this point happens in the chosen language and can be switched mid-way; earlier answers are kept as given. The hospital's name is shown at the top of every kiosk screen.

3.2 Agree: the care notice

  • Nothing is collected before the patient agrees. The notice explains that the answers go to the clinic team and that this is not a diagnosis.
  • Each notice has a version and a SHA-256 hash of its text; the server stores the answer with the version, hash, language, channel (kiosk, phone or nurse) and time, so a "no" is recorded as precisely as a "yes".
  • "No, I need staff help" ends the check-in and sends the patient to the desk.

3.3 Online AI help: a separate, optional question

What "yes" and "no" mean Yes: online services (Google Gemini, Sarvam AI) may turn the patient's voice into text, translate answers, read photos of old papers and prepare a draft for the doctor. The phone number is never sent.
No: the visit continues exactly the same way on this server alone. The patient taps and types, the read-back uses a voice installed on the device, and the doctor gets the rule-based case sheet with the patient's own words. The API enforces this, not just the screen. A "yes" can be changed until confirmation and withdrawn afterwards (chapter 4).

3.4 Details: age, sex and an optional phone

Age and sex shape the interview (no pregnancy questions for men or children) and the triage score. A mobile number is optional and is used only for SMS queue updates if the hospital connects an SMS provider. It is stored encrypted, with a keyed digest for lookup; a phone number never links records by itself, because families share phones.

Known defect, fixed first in Phase 7 (task 7.0.2)Typing a 10-digit number currently ticks the SMS opt-in box automatically. Consent must be the patient's own tap; the box will be unticked by default and SMS will become its own recorded consent purpose.

3.5 Returning patients: the OPD card

After age and sex the kiosk asks "Have you visited this hospital before?". "Yes" opens this card step; "No" goes straight to the safety check. A nurse-assisted check-in skips it, because the nurse links records at the desk.

If the card links, the kiosk shows only the date of the last visit and asks whether today continues that visit (a follow-up) or is a new problem. The rules behind the card are below.

  • Every patient gets an OPD number (format 26-000123: year and a per-hospital sequence) when their first visit is confirmed, and a six-digit PIN. The PIN is derived with the hospital's HMAC key and is never stored (section 16.8).
  • Entering both links today's visit to the existing record, provided today's age and sex fit it (birth year within one year; female/male must agree). A wrong number, wrong PIN or mismatch is refused without saying which part failed. Limits: 5 attempts per visit in 15 minutes, 10 per OPD number per hour. Every outcome is audited (without the number or PIN).
  • The shared kiosk never shows what an earlier visit was about: privacy on a public screen.
  • Without the card, a nurse can search and link at the desk (chapter 6). Records are never merged automatically.

3.6 Safety check: danger signs first

  • Tiles: chest discomfort, trouble breathing, heavy bleeding, fainting or unable to stay awake, sudden weakness or difficulty speaking, a seizure happening now.
  • Tapping a tile makes the visit urgent at once and raises an alert on every staff board of the department within a second (measured p95 under 1 s). The patient's row stays marked Nurse to confirm until a nurse acknowledges it after seeing them.
  • Get help now (stop the questions) opens an emergency screen telling the patient to go to the staff now; Continue lets them keep answering while staff come.
  • There is no anonymous "emergency" button: it was removed because anyone could raise false alarms. Real emergencies are handled by staff in person.

3.7 Questions: tap, speak or type

  • One question at a time, with a progress bar ("Question 1 / 10"). Tiles can be combined with the patient's own words.
  • The interview adapts. The main problem selects up to two symptom modules (fever, breathing, stomach, urine, pain, skin, injury, sleep and energy); then come past illnesses, operations, pregnancy (only where it can apply), medicines, allergies, family history, tobacco and alcohol, and "anything else". The full algorithm is in section 16.1.
  • AI follow-ups (with AI consent): up to three extra questions written for this complaint, in all three languages with tap options, checked like bank questions and labelled "AI follow-up" for the doctor.
  • Voice: the question is read aloud ("Listen" repeats it); a spoken answer is transcribed (Sarvam, then Gemini, then the browser's own recogniser) and shown for checking before it is used. Voice needs a secure page (HTTPS or the kiosk PC itself); otherwise the screen says so and offers tap and type.
  • Honest answers: "Don't know" and "Prefer not to say" are recorded as open questions, never as "no".
  • Prescription scan: on the medicines question the patient can photograph an old prescription; with AI consent the medicine names are read and shown back for confirmation. The photo is processed in memory and never stored.
  • Back changes the previous answer (it is shown pre-filled); changing the main problem re-plans the symptom questions. Stop and exit ends the check-in safely: a visit without a token is cancelled and never reaches the queue.
  • Every answer is saved at once as an encrypted interview turn, and re-runs the red-flag rules and the triage score. An answer sent twice (a double tap, a retry after lost Wi-Fi) is saved once; the kiosk says "tap again, your answers are kept".

3.8 Review: read-back and old papers

  • The whole history is read back in the patient's language. The read-back text is built from fixed templates, never by a language model.
  • Old papers (optional): the patient first taps what the paper is (prescription, test report, discharge paper, other), then photographs it. Up to five papers; each can be removed until confirmation. Only real JPEG, PNG or WebP photos up to 1 MB are kept, encrypted. Details in sections 7.4 and 16.10.
  • Yes, this is correct confirms. From then on the answers cannot be changed by the patient; staff can still correct the case sheet, with every edit versioned and audited.
  • If a patient stays on the review screen for 90 seconds without confirming, a nurse is asked to help.

3.9 Token, QR and OPD card

Confirmation (POST /intake/sessions/{id}/confirm) issues the day's token for the department (for example MED-003) exactly once, even if the request is repeated or sent twice at the same moment.

Print token prints a slip; Finish or 60 seconds of inactivity resets the kiosk for the next patient and clears everything from the browser. The QR opens the live status page (chapter 4); the OPD number and PIN let the patient link their next visit.

3.10 Help, comfort and accessibility

Always within reach

  • Call staff on every screen raises a "Help requested" alert on the staff board (limited to 5 presses per visit in 10 minutes, and not after the visit has finished).
  • Voice guidance reads every screen; one tap turns it off.
  • High contrast mode for low vision.
  • Every button is at least 64 px, easy for older hands.

Privacy on a shared screen

  • After 60 seconds without a tap the kiosk warns, then resets; the next patient never sees someone else's answers.
  • The kiosk keeps its patient session in tab-scoped storage only.
  • The shared screen never shows earlier visits' details.
  • The kiosk shows neutral wording only: no scores, no disease names.

3.11 When the kiosk has no network: the offline short form

If the kiosk loses its connection (and the page is in a secure context, which Web Crypto needs), the welcome screen offers a short form: complaint, duration, severity, long-term conditions, medicines, allergies and danger signs, with the same consent first. The form is encrypted in the browser's IndexedDB with AES-GCM under a non-extractable key, and the patient gets a provisional receipt. When the connection returns, the kiosk replays each form once to POST /intake/offline-submissions with its idempotency key, through the regular intake engine; staff then confirm the real token. Sending twice can never create two visits, and records are deleted from the device after a confirmed sync.

LimitThe offline form covers a kiosk that has lost its network. If the server itself is down, check-in stops; Phase 7 adds a paper fallback procedure (task 7.7.7).

3.12 Setting up a kiosk

  1. An administrator registers the kiosk in Settings → Kiosks and gets a one-time activation code valid for 24 hours, with Copy setup link.
  2. Opening the link once on the kiosk device (/kiosk?activate=…) exchanges the code for a long-lived device secret; from then on the kiosk signs itself in (12-hour kiosk tokens, renewed with the secret), overnight and after restarts.
  3. The kiosk sends a heartbeat about once a minute, so Settings shows it as Online, Offline, Not seen yet or Turned off.
  4. A replaced device gets a New activation code for the same kiosk record; its history is kept and the old device is signed out once the new one pairs. Deactivate pauses it; Delete cuts it off at once.

4 Patients' own phones

Patients can check in on their own phones from a QR poster, and every patient can follow their turn live from the QR on their token. No app and no login are needed.

4.1 Phone check-in from a QR poster

  • An administrator creates a Phone check-in QR (Settings → Kiosks → Type) and prints it for the waiting hall. The code inside is derived from the hospital key; Replace QR makes old posters stop working at once; Deactivate pauses phone check-in.
  • The patient completes the same server-side interview as at a kiosk, with the same consent, safety rules and token. Staff see a Phone badge on these patients so the desk can greet them.
  • Reloading the page or losing Wi-Fi resumes the same visit at the same question. After the token is issued, reopening the link shows "You already have token MED-012" with a button to the live page and an option to check in someone else.
  • Abuse limits: one phone can start at most 6 check-ins per QR in 10 minutes, one QR at most 150 in 10 minutes; patients enter the queue only after they confirm.
  • Over plain HTTP on the hospital Wi-Fi the microphone is not available, so the page says "voice needs the hospital's secure link" and offers tapping and typing. HTTPS is a Phase 7 task (7.2.1).

4.2 The live status page

  • What it shows: the token and department, people ahead, an estimated wait as a five-minute range (never a false-precise time, section 16.5), a four-step progress track (Waiting → Called → With doctor → Done), and the language switch. It updates by itself over a server-sent event stream, falling back to polling.
  • When called the phone vibrates and the tab title changes, so the moment is not missed.
  • Missed call: if staff mark the patient absent, the page asks them to go to the desk; their token and place are kept for a recall.
  • Stop AI help: a patient who said yes can withdraw from here. The link can only stop AI help, never start it, so a photographed token slip cannot switch it on. From then on nothing more about the visit goes to AI services, the doctor no longer sees the AI draft, and it cannot be reviewed.
  • Privacy: the address contains a 256-bit opaque token derived from the hospital key and stored only as a hash; the page shows no name or health details.
  • SMS (optional): if the hospital connects a provider and the patient opts in, they get their token and messages when 5 people, 2 people and 0 people are ahead of them (section 16.11). Off by default.

5 The staff workspace

Nurses, doctors and administrators use one workspace at /staff. It works on laptops, tablets and phones; on a phone the sidebar becomes a top bar.

Signing in

  • Staff choose their hospital from a picker (the server lists hospital names only), then enter email and password. Passwords are at least 12 characters and stored as Argon2id hashes.
  • Five wrong passwords lock the account for 15 minutes; sign-in is also rate-limited per address. Every success, failure and lockout is in the audit log.
  • A successful sign-in gives a 10-minute access token (kept in memory) and an 8-hour refresh cookie (HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict, scoped to the sign-in path). The browser renews the access token silently, so a shift needs one sign-in.
  • A deactivated or deleted account loses access within 10 minutes, and an open queue board closes within a minute.
  • Use another account lets a second person sign in on a shared PC; each browser tab keeps its own account.

The triage score, explained on the page

The score (0–100) adds points for danger signs and red flags, warning answers, age and pregnancy, recent onset, and, lightly, the patient's own 0–10 rating. Urgent patients go first; everyone else keeps arrival order with a head start of 0.6 minutes per point, never more than 45. The full rules and a worked example are in sections 16.3 and 16.4.

Linking a returning patient at the desk

A patient who forgot their OPD card can be linked by a nurse: search by OPD number or phone number (results show OPD number, age, sex, visit count and last visit date, never names or clinical details), confirm identity in person, then link. A record created at check-in for someone who registered as new is emptied and its number retired. Linking is refused when both records already have history: there is no automatic merge. The search (patient.lookup) and the link (queue.patient_linked) are audited, and the doctor's view shows that staff made the link.

Live and reliable

  • Updates arrive over a WebSocket within a second; the board renders a 5,000-patient list and applies an urgent change in well under 100 ms (virtualised rows).
  • A board whose sign-in expires, or whose account is deactivated, is closed by the server (4401/4403) and reconnects after renewing the sign-in.
  • Visits left open on an earlier day are closed automatically after midnight, so every morning starts with an empty queue (section 16.14).

7.2 Overview and the AI draft

How the AI draft is reviewed
  • The model writes the brief as sentences, each citing the question_ids of the answers that support it. The server keeps only citations to questions this patient was actually asked and drops any sentence left without one; the output guard checks every sentence.
  • A chip opens that answer in the transcript, highlighted. "Compare with original answers" does the same for the whole brief.
  • The doctor records Accept, Edit (their own words; the AI wording is kept for the record) or Reject. The decision is versioned (If-Match), audited and limited to doctors of the treating department.
  • The PDF says who reviewed it and never prints a rejected draft. A background AI step never overwrites a reviewed summary. If the patient withdraws AI consent, the draft disappears and cannot be reviewed.

Below the draft come the presenting complaint (complaint, since when, severity, site, character, associated symptoms, denials) and the key history points (past illnesses, operations, medicines, allergies, family, tobacco and alcohol, open questions), each with "See full history".

The consultation workflow card follows the visit's real state: call the patient → consultation → complete. Under it, the queue timeline lists every change to the visit, who made it and when: token issued by the patient, called by the doctor, marked absent by a nurse, recalled, called again, consultation started. The timeline is append-only: the application can add entries but never change or delete them (GET /visits/{id}/queue-events, staff of the visit's department only).

When the patient said no to AI help, the overview says so plainly: the sheet comes from the rules alone and the answers stay in the patient's own words (figure 7.1).

7.3 Patient history: every field, with its source

  • Sections: presenting complaint; history of present illness (since, severity, site, character, associated symptoms, denials); past illnesses and operations; medicines; allergies; family history; tobacco, alcohol and other habits; pregnancy where asked; open questions ("Does not know: …", "Preferred not to say: …").
  • Edit any field. A save sends a restricted JSON patch with the version it was based on; every save creates a new version and an audit entry, and staff edits are marked Staff-verified. If two people edit at once, the second is told and can reload or re-apply their change; nothing is silently overwritten. Leaving with an unsaved edit asks first.

7.4 Previous visits

Up to 20 earlier visits at the same hospital: date, department, token, complaint, red flags and the AI brief (marked when staff edited the sheet), plus allergies, medicines and conditions with the date each was last reported, labelled "patient-reported, confirm today". Doctors and nurses see it only through a visit of their own department that is still open or took place today; once that visit is closed and in the past the server answers HISTORY_CLOSED. Administrators can read it through any visit. Every read is audited (patient_history.read). Records are never shared between hospitals.

7.5 Old papers

  • Papers from this visit and, while the patient is under care, from earlier visits, newest first by the date printed on them. A doctor or nurse can photograph more (Kind of paper → Photograph a paper), up to 10 per visit.
  • With AI consent, each paper has an AI reading: the source (laboratory or doctor), diagnoses and medicines as written, test results with value, unit and the range printed beside them, procedures and the printed date.
  • Compared is plain arithmetic against the printed range on the same paper: "Below the printed range", "Within", "Above", plus a high/low mark printed by the laboratory. Nothing is flagged from a general range or by the model (section 16.10).
  • The doctor records Matches the photo or Does not match (doctors only, changeable, audited). A rejected reading is no longer shown. Without AI consent the photo is kept unread: "Not read by AI. Read the photo."
  • Photos are sent with Cache-Control: no-store, and every view of a photo is audited.

7.6 Ayurveda

The patient answers six questions at the kiosk: appetite (Ahara Shakti), digestion (Jarana Shakti), bowel habit (Mala), sleep (Nidra), capacity for exertion (Vyayama Shakti) and diet. The clinician records 16 examination findings that are never asked of the patient: Ashtavidha Pariksha (Nadi, Mutra, Mala, Jihva, Shabda, Sparsha, Drik, Akriti) and Dashavidha items (Prakriti, Vikriti, Sara, Samhanana, Pramana, Satmya, Sattva, Vaya). Everything is labelled a draft for the vaidya's review.

7.7 Interview transcript and clinical notes

Clinical notes holds the doctor's own examination findings, assessment, plan and follow-up date, written by the doctor and never by AI. Each save is a new version, the earlier versions stay readable, and a save that does not start from the latest version is refused, so two doctors cannot overwrite each other. Doctors of the visit's department can write notes while the patient is under care (the visit is open, or it is today's); nurses and administrators read them. The latest version is printed on the case sheet PDF, apart from the history. Below the notes are staff edits to the case sheet, its version, and the corrections the patient made before confirming.

7.8 The PDF case sheet

PDF or Print always produces the full case sheet, whichever tab is open: a compact, multilingual, history-only document rendered on the server (WeasyPrint, with Inter and Noto fonts so Hindi and Marathi print correctly). It includes the reviewed AI summary with the reviewer's name (never a rejected draft), open questions, the Ayurveda section when present, and the disclaimer "History only. Not a diagnosis."

Guard-rails on every AI answer

  • Requests ask for JSON that matches a schema; the answer is re-validated against the schema and the application's own models.

12 One system, many hospitals

Recommended: one installation per hospital

  • A server or PC inside the hospital runs PostgreSQL, Redis, the API, the worker and the web app with Docker.
  • Patient data stays in the building; check-in and the queue keep working when the internet is down.
  • Each installation has its own passwords and encryption keys.
  • Installed with one script, setup-hospital.ps1 (chapter 23).

Also possible: several hospitals on one server

  • For example a district hosting its primary health centres: setup-hospital.ps1 -AddHospital.
  • Staff pick their hospital at sign-in and see only their own.
  • Separation is enforced by PostgreSQL row-level security, not only by the application: every tenant table refuses rows of another hospital.

How many kiosks?

As many as the hospital needs. Every kiosk and phone QR belongs to one hospital and feeds that hospital's department queues: 100 kiosks still mean one General Medicine queue, in fair calling order. Tokens are numbered per department per day (MED-001, MED-002…) whichever kiosk the patient used.

A second hospital was set up and checked live: its patient appeared only in its own queue, and neither hospital could open, call, change or delete the other's patients, staff or kiosks (20 of 20 checks). The automated isolation tests probe every route that names a record as another hospital, another department and another patient (section 19.5).

13.2 What happens when a patient confirms

Figure 13.2 Confirmation issues the token in PostgreSQL first; Redis and the live screens follow. The worker's outbox relay repairs anything a crash in between could miss.
  • PostgreSQL first. A token exists only once the transaction commits. A repeated or simultaneous confirmation returns the same token (the receipt is rebuilt from the database if the Redis session was lost).
  • The outbox pattern. Every queue change writes an outbox row in the same transaction. The worker relays unprocessed rows every 2 seconds (FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED, up to 100 at a time), so the Redis read model converges even if the API crashed after committing.
  • Sequenced deltas. Each department's queue has a sequence number incremented inside the same Lua script as the change; a board that sees a gap asks for a fresh snapshot.
  • Rebuildable. If Redis is lost, the API or worker rebuilds every department's sorted set from today's queued visits at start-up (guarded by a lock), and cli queue-rebuild does it on demand.

16 Algorithms and rules

Every decision OPD Saathi makes about a patient comes from deterministic, readable rules in the domain package: no network, no database, no AI. AI output enters only as validated data and can only add caution. All rule files are marked draft_needs_clinician_review, and the loaders refuse them otherwise.

16.1 The interview engine

services/api/app/domain/interview/engine.py holds the interview as an immutable state (language, queued and asked question ids, answers, red-flag ids, AI questions, sex, age, extra modules). Every function returns a new state; the API stores it in the Redis session and every answer also as an encrypted interview_turns row.

Figure 16.1 The order in which questions are planned.

Planning the questions

  1. Core. Every interview starts with chief_concern (11 tiles plus free text), duration (today, 1–3 days, 4–7 days, 1–4 weeks, more than a month) and severity (0–10 scale).
  2. Symptom modules. When the main problem is answered, its text (NFKC-normalised, case-folded) is matched against keyword lists in bank/triggers.json; up to two modules are chosen, in bank order. Some phrases are ignored per module so they do not trigger wrongly (for example "fell ill" or "hair fall" never trigger the injury module).
  3. History, always: past illnesses (with operations and pregnancy), medicines, allergies, family history, personal history (tobacco, alcohol), and the closing "anything else". A hospital's optional modules (Ayurveda) go just before the closing question.
  4. AI follow-ups (only with AI consent and the hospital switch): when they arrive, up to 3 are inserted before the first unanswered history question, only if there is room under the cap; otherwise the engine records "skipped" and continues with the bank.

Which questions a patient actually sees

A question (and each of its options) can declare who it applies to: only_sexes, min_age/max_age, hide_with_modules (skip when another module covers it) and hide_with_answers (skip when an earlier answer makes it pointless). A question whose real options are all hidden is skipped; one listed under skip_if_only_concerns is skipped when the patient only tapped those concerns. The progress bar shows answered + remaining, never more than the cap.

Answers

  • Validation: a scale answer must be a whole number 0–10; text has control characters removed, whitespace collapsed and at most 500 characters; required questions cannot be empty; optional ones can be skipped. An answer must be for the current question, otherwise it is refused (the API first checks whether it already saved that exact answer, which makes retries safe).
  • Back removes the last answer and returns it for pre-filling; going back to the main problem forgets the symptom questions and AI follow-ups planned from it.
  • Correction on the review screen replaces an answer. If a changed main problem plans different modules, answers that no longer fit are dropped and new questions become pending; AI follow-ups written for the old problem go too.
  • Answers are fixed once the database records a token; the check is against the database, not the session.

16.2 The red-flag engine

domain/redflags/engine.py evaluates 15 rules from rules.yaml against the patient's text and facts after every answer. Results are deduplicated, ordered and stored once per visit and rule (red_flag_events); an urgent flag raises a staff alert at once.

Matching, step by step

  1. Normalise the text: Unicode NFKC and case-folding; Devanagari nukta removed (so precomposed and decomposed spellings match); chandrabindu treated as anusvara (common informal typing); punctuation becomes spaces; whitespace collapsed. Then split into tokens.
  2. Check the rule's structured conditions against the patient's facts: tile_any (a danger tile tapped), min_age/max_age, sex_any, pregnant, severity_min. A rule whose conditions fail is skipped.
  3. Match phrase groups. A rule has one or more groups; each group lists phrases in English, Hindi, Marathi and Hinglish (the loader refuses a group missing a language). Every group must match (AND); within a group any phrase may match (OR). A phrase matches as a whole token sequence.
  4. Negation. A match is ignored when a negation word (no, not, without, nahi, nahin, nhi, नहीं, नही, नाही…) appears in the 3 tokens before it, or in the 3 tokens after it unless a contrast (but, however, लेकिन, पण…) or conjunction (and, और, आणि, aur, ani) comes between.
  5. Evidence. The flag records the tapped tile, or two words either side of the earliest match (at most 240 characters), so staff read exactly what triggered it.
Example: negation"I have no chest pain" → the phrase "chest pain" has "no" within 3 tokens before it → no flag.
Example: two groups"chest pain, not sweating" → group 1 ("chest pain") matches, group 2 ("sweating") is negated → RF_CHEST_PAIN_ASSOC (urgent) does not fire; RF_CHEST_PAIN (priority) does.

16.4 Calling order and the head start

domain/queue/scoring.py turns the level and score into a virtual arrival time; lower is called first.

head_start (minutes) = min(45, round(score × 0.6))
  routine: capped at 17 (just below the priority floor of round(30 × 0.6) = 18)
  priority: at least 18
virtual_arrival = queued_at − head_start
calling order = (urgent first, virtual_arrival, visit id)
  • Urgent patients are always first, however long others have waited.
  • Bounding the head start by level means a nurse's change of level always wins over the points.
  • A routine patient can only be overtaken by someone who arrived less than 45 minutes after them: nobody waits for ever.
  • queued_at is kept through a recall, so a recalled patient returns to their original place. Ties are broken by visit id, which is a time-ordered UUIDv7. Visits from before the triage score existed use fixed bonuses (urgent 360, priority 45, vulnerable +15 minutes).

16.5 The wait estimate

consult = average of the last 20 consultations once there are 5, else 4 minutes; kept within 1–15
baseline = (position − 1) × consult ÷ max(1, active doctors)
shown range = [ceil(0.75 × baseline ÷ 5) × 5 , ceil(1.25 × baseline ÷ 5) × 5] minutes

Example: 5th in line, 2 doctors, 6-minute consultations → baseline 12 → "10–15 min". A five-minute range avoids a falsely precise promise.

16.6 Department routing

domain/routing/engine.py chooses a department from the patient's stated concern by keyword modules, and sends anything unclear to a nurse.

  1. Surgery first: "abdominal lump", "stomach lump", "hernia" (and Hindi/Marathi equivalents) → SURG, confidence 0.90.
  2. Match every module (ORTHO, ENT, OBG, DERM, OPHTH, SURG, GENMED) on the normalised text; each module contributes its earliest matching phrase. OBG counts only for a woman aged 12–50 or a pregnant patient.

  4. No match → NURSE_TRIAGE, 0.0, needs nurse confirmation.
  5. Otherwise the module whose phrase appears earliest in the text wins: confidence 0.90 if only one department matched, 0.65 if several did. Below 0.70 the queue row shows "Department not confirmed" for a nurse.

16.9 The AI pipeline

Figure 16.3 Every language-model call goes through the same pipeline (ai/providers/llm/gemini.py).
  • Deadline and retry: each call has a total deadline (callers set it; at most 20 s). The first attempt gets 65% of it; a retryable failure (timeout, connection error, HTTP 408, 429 or 5xx) retries once on the fallback model after 0.1–0.3 s of jitter.

16.10 Old papers: reading and printed-range flags

  • Accepted files: JPEG, PNG or WebP whose first bytes match the declared type, at most 1,000,000 bytes (the kiosk sends a ~1280 px JPEG of about 200 kB). Up to 5 per check-in by the patient, 10 per visit in all. Each photo is encrypted with AES-256-GCM bound to its document id, so a photo copied onto another record cannot be decrypted.
  • Reading runs in the background (25 s budget) only with AI consent; a reading still pending after 2 minutes is shown as failed. The model is told to copy what is clearly written and not to interpret.
  • Flags by arithmetic (domain/documents.py):
value : a number, optionally followed by its unit ("9.8 g/dL", "2,10,000 /cumm")
range : "12 - 15", "12–15", "12 to 15", "(4,000 - 11,000)", "< 5.7", "up to 5", "> 40"
digit grouping: "1,50,000" and "150,000" are both 150000
flag : above if value > high · below if value < low · within otherwise (a value at a limit is within)
no flag: text results ("No growth"), censored values ("< 0.5"), several ranges, reversed ranges, anything unparsed

A high or low mark printed by the laboratory ("H", "LOW", ↑, ↓) is shown as printed, separately. No general reference range is ever applied, because ranges differ between laboratories, methods, ages and sexes. A printed date is used only if it is a real date between 1950 and today.

16.11 Notifications

  • After every relayed queue change, the worker looks at the first six patients and records one-time intents: token_issued, and five_ahead, two_ahead, your_turn when a patient reaches position 6, 3 and 1. Only visits with SMS opt-in and a phone get intents, deduplicated per visit and template.
  • At most 5 messages per recipient per hour (serialised with an advisory lock on the phone digest). Delivery retries after 1, 5 and 30 seconds, then the message is marked failed. Messages carry only the token and position, in the patient's language, and the phone number is never logged.
  • Providers: console (default, logs only), Twilio SMS, WhatsApp Cloud API.
Known defect (Phase 7 task 7.0.1)The worker deliberately has no patient-data key, yet delivery decrypts the phone number in the worker. On the demo stack this makes the notification step fail every 2 seconds once a patient has opted in; queue updates still work because they commit first. The fix gives the worker a separate, narrower key for opted-in phone numbers only.

16.12 Reports

MechanismHow it works
Sliding-window limitsA Lua script on Redis keeps a sorted set of request times per key, trims the window, and answers allowed / remaining / retry-after (Retry-After, X-RateLimit-* headers). The sign-in limit fails closed if Redis is down; the others fall back to a bounded in-process window. Session lockOne change at a time per patient session (Redis lock, 20 s), so a double tap cannot store two answers; a re-sent answer that was already saved returns where the interview stands.
Optimistic concurrencyCase-sheet edits and AI reviews send If-Match with the version (ETag); a stale version gets 409 and the screen offers reload or re-apply.

19 Security and privacy

AuthorisationAn explicit public-route allow-list in middleware; per-route roles; department checks on every visit route and queue action; the earlier-records rule (open or today's visit); a patient session reaches only its own visit and can change it only until the token is issued.
Tenant isolationPostgreSQL row-level security, forced, on every tenant table, scoped per transaction; composite tenant foreign keys; secret lookups before the tenant is known go through narrow, exact-match policies.
Data at restAES-256-GCM for names, phones, ABHA numbers, interview text, case sheets, paper readings and photos, each bound by associated data to its table, column and row (patients:phone_enc:<id>), so ciphertext moved to another row fails to decrypt. IntegrityIdempotent writes; optimistic versions; append-only audit log and visit timeline (no update or delete grant for the app); transactional outbox.
AbuseRate limits on every public and patient route; 1 MiB request limit; strict request models that reject unknown fields; file-signature checks on photos; "Call staff" and phone check-in limits.
ConfigurationIn production the settings refuse development signing keys, short secrets and malformed 32-byte keys, enforce the 10-minute access-token lifetime and hide the API docs. The setup scripts generate random secrets and never reuse published defaults.
Supply chainPython dependencies installed with --require-hashes from a lock file; npm ci; pip-audit, npm audit, Bandit, Semgrep (0 findings) and Gitleaks on every change; the API image runs as a non-root user; an SBOM on request (make sbom).

19.3 Consent and the patient's control

  • Care consent is required; AI consent is optional and is asked separately with nothing pre-selected. Each is stored with the notice's version and text hash, language, channel and time.
  • The AI answer can be changed until confirmation and withdrawn afterwards by the patient (status page) or by staff. Withdrawal stops all further AI processing, discards work still running, hides the AI draft and paper readings, and blocks their review. Processing done before the withdrawal is not undone.
  • Without AI consent, nothing about the visit leaves the server: no model call, no online speech recognition, no browser speech recognition. This is checked in tests by counting calls that reach a stub provider.

19.4 What is audited

Sign-in success, failure and lockout; every queue action (queue.called, queue.skipped, queue.recalled, queue.override…); alert acknowledgements; case-sheet reads and edits; AI reviews; history reads (patient_history.read); patient lookups and links; paper list views, photo views, additions, removals and reviews; consent answers and withdrawals; OPD-card attempts (outcome only); every administrator change; report views. Audit metadata never contains patient answers or credentials.

19.5 How isolation is tested

tests/integration/test_access_isolation.py keeps an inventory of every route that names a record. Each is probed as another hospital's staff, another department's staff and another patient's session (same and other hospital): each must answer "not found", change nothing and leave nothing in the owner's audit trail. Adding a route without a probe fails the test. These probes and the 15 scenarios found 8 defects, all fixed with a test that failed first (chapter 21).

22 Running the demo

The demo is a complete fictional hospital started by one script on a Windows PC with Docker Desktop. It must never be used for a real hospital.

# from the repository root, with Docker Desktop running
powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\start-demo.ps1          # first run builds images (several minutes)
powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\start-demo.ps1 -NoBuild # later runs, seconds
powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\scripts\demo-check.ps1  # is everything ready?
powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\scripts\lan-check.ps1   # can phones reach this PC?

What start-demo.ps1 does

  1. Checks Docker; creates .env with random secrets if missing (and fills in signing keys an older .env lacks, so the published development defaults are never used).
  2. Picks the web port (5173, or 8080 if busy) and this PC's Wi-Fi/LAN address for the QR links (PUBLIC_WEB_URL), and opens the web port to the network.
  3. Starts PostgreSQL and Redis, applies migrations, seeds the Demo Government Hospital, Nagpur: 8 departments, one doctor per department (doctor01…doctor08), five General Medicine nurses (nurse01…nurse05) and an administrator, all @demo.opdsathi.test.
  4. Adds a believable month of finished OPD days for Reports, and resets the fictional returning patient (Sunita, 52, with an OPD card, an earlier visit three weeks ago and an old prescription photo kept unread).
  5. Starts the API, worker and web app; creates a kiosk activation link and a phone check-in link; prints the staff logins and the returning patient's OPD number and PIN; opens the kiosk in the default browser.
PasswordsDemo passwords live only in .env (DEMO_ADMIN_PASSWORD, DEMO_STAFF_PASSWORD) and are printed by the script; they are deliberately not written in any document.

demo-check.ps1 checks the services, readiness, the sign-in hospital list, the database migration level, which AI and voice providers were selected, a tiny live answer from Gemini and Sarvam (no patient data), the returning patient, and the synthetic papers in docs/demo-papers/. Run start-demo.ps1 again after every reboot or network change: QR codes must point at the PC's current address.

23 Installing at a hospital

Each hospital gets its own installation: a PC or server inside the hospital network with Docker, its own database and its own keys. Read chapter 26 first: today's build is ready for supervised pilots with synthetic data, not for real patients.

cd C:\OPD-Saathi
Set-ExecutionPolicy -Scope Process -ExecutionPolicy Bypass
.\setup-hospital.ps1                 # first installation; safe to run again (keeps keys and passwords)
.\setup-hospital.ps1 -NoBuild        # restart quickly after editing .env
.\setup-hospital.ps1 -AddHospital    # add another hospital on the same server
.\setup-hospital.ps1 -WebPort 8080   # use another port

The script creates .env with fresh random passwords and keys, builds and starts the stack, applies migrations, asks for the hospital's name, a short unique code and the first administrator, creates them with the 8 standard departments, and prints the addresses for staff PCs and kiosks. The administrator's first day

  1. Sign in at /staff using the server's network address (not localhost), and open Settings.
  2. Departments: keep, rename, deactivate or delete the standard ones; add others; set the call grace period; decide on the Ayurveda history.
  3. Users: add every doctor and nurse with their department.
  4. Kiosks: register each kiosk and open its setup link once on that device (Chrome or Edge, F11); create a phone check-in QR if patients will use their own phones.
  5. Take the first backup and store .env safely with it (without APP_DATA_KEY the encrypted fields can never be read).

  1. Take a backup and keep a copy of .env.
  2. Replace the folder with the new version (or git pull), keeping .env.

  4. Open tabs pick up the new version on their next reload.

26 Limitations and the road to production

Every feature in the IIT Bombay plan is built and tested, and the system runs reliably as a demonstration on synthetic data. It is not ready for real patients yet. The final phase, Phase 7, is planned in docs/PHASE_7_PRODUCTION_PLAN.md: 17 workstreams and 131 tasks, about 12 weeks for three engineers, with clinical and legal tracks in parallel.

What stands between today and a real hospital

Needs people outside the team

  • Clinical sign-off of every question, rule, weight and Ayurveda item; native-speaker review of Hindi and Marathi.
  • DPDP Act: patient notice, processor agreements, retention schedule, breach procedure; a regulatory (medical-device) assessment.
  • Paid or enterprise AI contracts with data-processing terms, or AI switched off.
  • Hospital IT: certificates, network segmentation, backup storage, a pilot site.

Engineering

  • HTTPS; a production configuration that cannot fall back to development secrets.
  • Password reset, MFA for administrators, key rotation and escrow.
  • Encrypted, off-site, point-in-time backups with restore drills; monitoring that alerts a person.
  • Image scanning, DAST, API fuzzing, an independent penetration test; real load and 8-hour soak tests.
