Start here

The system on one page

Government OPDs see hundreds of patients a morning. Patients speak different languages, describe problems in their own words and wait without knowing their turn; doctors get a few minutes each and spend much of it collecting basic history.

What OPD Saathi does

  • Collects the patient's history before the consultation, in their language, at a kiosk or on their own phone, by tapping, speaking or typing.
  • Spots danger signs first and gives every patient a fair, explained place in the queue.
  • Gives the doctor an English case sheet with the patient's own words, earlier visits and old test reports beside it.
  • Shows staff a live queue and patients a live status page on their phone.
  • Gives administrators reports, an audit log and control of staff, kiosks, departments and features.

What it deliberately does not do

  • It never diagnoses, names a disease, suggests a medicine or dose, or checks drug interactions.
  • AI only drafts and double-checks: every AI line is labelled, cites the patient's answers and is accepted or rejected by a doctor. AI can raise caution, never lower it.
  • Patients never see a score; staff stay responsible for who is seen first.
  • Clinical wording and translations are drafts until clinicians and native speakers sign them off.

No internet?

The kiosk keeps an encrypted short form and sends it once the network is back; the history still builds from rules.

No AI?

Tapping, typing, recorded voice prompts, the rules engine and the bilingual question bank do the whole visit (page 13).

Server down?

The printed paper fallback pack (forms in three languages, a token roll) keeps the OPD going.

1 · Patients

The patient interview, screen by screen

A touch-screen kiosk (any tablet or PC with Chrome or Edge) or the patient's own phone. Six steps show at the top of every screen: Agree → Details → Safety check → Questions → Review → Token.

Made for everyone

Voice guidance, buttons of at least 64 px, larger text and high contrast; Call staff on every screen; Back and Stop at any time. An idle kiosk warns, then clears itself after 60 seconds.

Answers are honest

"Don't know" and "Prefer not to say" stay open questions, never a "no". Voice keeps the patient's own words beside the English, with how sure the speech recognition was.

Same on a phone

The poster QR opens these screens on the patient's phone, with the same consent, safety rules and token; a reload or lost Wi-Fi resumes the same question.

2 · The interview

Every question we ask

33 questions in 16 topics, each in Hindi, Marathi and English with one-tap answers, and open to speech or typing. A patient sees 8–15: the core three, up to two topics chosen from the complaint, and the background topics.

Before any question: the safety check

Chest discomfort · Trouble breathing · Heavy bleeding · Fainting or unable to stay awake · Sudden weakness or difficulty speaking · A seizure happening now. Any tap makes the visit urgent and alerts the staff.

1 · Patients (continued)

The OPD card, the patient's phone, and their rights

What the OPD card is

An OPD number (for example 26-000001) and a six-digit PIN, printed on the token slip at the end of check-in. The number stays with the patient at that hospital; the PIN is worked out from a secret each time and never stored.

How it is used

  • At the kiosk: "Yes, I have my OPD card", then the number and PIN. Today's age and sex must fit the record; wrong tries are limited and audited; the shared kiosk never shows what an earlier visit was about. The patient says whether today is a follow-up or a new problem.
  • At the desk: a nurse searches by OPD number or phone (results show number, age, sex and visit count — no names), confirms in person, and links the visit.
  • Lost card: the nurse re-issues it with a new PIN; the number and the records stay, and the old PIN stops working at once.
  • For the doctor: the visit shows the patient's Previous visits (page 9).

A printed PIN is not strong enough for patients to open their own records, so it only links visits (ADR 014).

Phone check-in

An administrator prints a Phone check-in QR (Settings → Kiosks). Patients answer the same interview with the same rules; staff see a Phone badge. "Replace QR" retires old posters; the hospital can switch it off.

The live status page

From the QR on the token: the token, people ahead, a five-minute wait range (never a falsely exact time) and Waiting → Called → With doctor → Done. The phone vibrates when called. No name or health details appear.

Rights and the notice

What is collected, why, who sees it, how long it is kept, the patient's rights and where to complain, in three languages, with the hospital's grievance officer and a printable poster. AI help and SMS can be stopped from the phone.

SMS and WhatsApp (token, then 5, 2 and 0 people ahead) are built but stay off until the hospital has a provider and registered templates (DLT). Only the patient's own tick asks for them.
3 · Nurses

The live queue

Live and reliable

  • Changes reach every board within a second; a 5,000-patient list applies an urgent change in under 100 ms.
  • A board whose sign-in ends is closed by the server; yesterday's open visits close themselves after midnight.

What is on the board

  • Counters (waiting, called, in consultation, urgent) that filter; department, status and search filters.
  • Alerts, newest first, each with Acknowledge: Urgent danger sign or red flag, Help requested, and Priority "AI suggests… please confirm", always quoting the patient. Unacknowledged urgent alerts sound again every 60 seconds.
  • Patients in calling order: token, age and sex, complaint in English, priority with score and Why?, waiting time, status.
  • Next to call with a one-click Call next.

Calling, fairly and safely

  • One patient per doctor: a doctor's call reserves the patient; two doctors pressing Call next together get two different patients.
  • Missed calls: "Mark absent" unlocks after the hospital's grace period (3 minutes by default, counted down). The patient keeps token, urgency and arrival time; Recall puts them back in place; Put back undoes a wrong call; No-show only after a miss.
  • Nurse to confirm stays on a self-reported danger sign until a nurse has seen the patient.
  • Department and priority changes need a written reason; every action is checked against the person's department.
  • Board in Hindi and Marathi; alerts and Call next work with Tab and Enter alone.
The sickest patients surface in seconds; a patient who stepped out is not punished; two doctors never call the same person; and every call, absence and override says who did it.
3 · Nurses (continued)

How the fair queue works

The score (0–100), staff only

  • Points come from objective signs: an urgent red flag or danger tile 70, a priority flag 30, warning answers up to 25, vulnerability (infant +20, aged 75+ +12, pregnant +12), started today +6.
  • The patient's own 0–10 rating adds at most 4 — typing "10" cannot jump the queue.
  • Routine under 30 · Priority 30+ · Urgent 70+ or any urgent red flag.

The calling order

  • Urgent patients go first. Everyone else keeps arrival order with a head start of 0.6 minutes per point, never more than 45 — so nobody waits for ever.
  • AI is a checker, not a decider: it may raise a "please confirm" alert, never lowers urgency, and alone counts as Priority at most.
  • Weights and the 15 red-flag rules are readable files marked "draft — needs clinician review".

The Patients page: every visit, and help at the desk

Register

Every visit for today, 7 or 30 days, with status tiles, filters, search and "Open case sheet" — found in milliseconds even with years of visits.

Assisted check-in

A nurse checks in a patient who cannot use the kiosk; consent is still shown first and recorded as given at the desk, then the same interview, rules and queue follow.

Returning patients at the desk

Search by OPD number or phone, confirm in person, then link; re-issue a lost card with a new PIN (page 6).

Anyone can ask "why was that person called before me?" and get a line-by-line answer; nobody is left out because they cannot use the kiosk or forgot their card.
4 · Doctors

The case sheet, with its sources

Left: today's patients. Right: one patient in tabs — Overview · Patient history · Previous visits · Old papers · Ayurveda · Interview transcript · Clinical notes — so urgent warnings and the AI draft to check come first and the details are one click away.

Header

Token, age and sex, department, language, case-sheet version, status, priority with Why?, and one main action that follows the visit (Call → Start → Finish), plus PDF. "Answered by a parent or guardian" when someone else gave the history.

The AI draft: a sentence, its sources, a decision

  • The model writes the brief as sentences that each cite the questions they rest on; the server drops any sentence citing nothing the patient was asked, and the output guard checks every one.
  • A chip opens that answer in the transcript, highlighted; "Compare with original answers" does the same for the whole brief.
  • The doctor records Accept, Edit (their words; the AI wording is kept for the record) or Reject — versioned and audited. The PDF names the reviewer and never prints a rejected draft.

Patient history and the transcript

Every field shows the English text, the patient's own words and how they gave it (tap, voice with confidence, typed). Edits create versions marked Staff-verified; two people editing at once cannot overwrite each other. The transcript of every question and answer cannot be changed.

Previous visits

Up to 20 earlier visits at the same hospital: date, department, complaint, red flags and the reviewed AI brief, plus allergies, medicines and conditions with the date each was last reported — labelled "patient-reported, confirm today". Doctors and nurses see it only while the patient is under their department's care today.

Visit timeline

Every change to the visit, who made it and when: token issued, called, marked absent, recalled, consultation started. The application can add lines but never change or delete them.

The doctor starts with a checked summary instead of a blank page, sees exactly which answer each AI sentence came from, and knows the patient's past without asking again.
4 · Doctors (continued)

Old papers, results over time and the doctor's own notes

Old papers

  • Photos from the kiosk, and up to 10 a visit added by staff, newest first by the date printed on them; earlier visits' papers while the patient is under care.
  • With AI consent, each paper gets a reading: source, diagnoses and medicines as written, results with value, unit and the printed range.
  • "Below / Within / Above the printed range" is plain arithmetic against that paper's own range, plus the lab's own high/low mark — never a general range, never the model's opinion.
  • The doctor marks Matches the photo or Does not match. Without AI consent the photo is kept unread. Every view of a photo is audited.

Results over time

Only readings a doctor accepted, from this and earlier visits, one table per test, each result next to the range printed on its own paper. A trend that would take minutes to find in a pile of papers is one glance.

FHIR export

A visit can be exported as a FHIR R4 bundle (patient, encounter, complaint and conditions as unconfirmed, allergies, medicines as stated, accepted results, the interview, consents and who answered) through the API, for connecting to a hospital system. It is marked "not ABDM-certified".

The doctor's own notes

  • Examination findings, assessment, plan and a follow-up date, written by the doctor and never by AI.
  • Each save is a new version; earlier ones stay readable; a save that does not start from the latest version is refused, so two doctors cannot overwrite each other.
  • The latest version is printed on the case-sheet PDF.

PDF and speed

PDF / Print always gives the full history-only case sheet in three scripts, with the reviewed AI summary, open questions and Ayurveda findings. A whole visit — call, start, accept the draft, write notes, finish, print — takes 6 clicks and one key.

5 · Administrators

Reports, settings, the audit log and the System page

Reports

For today, 7, 30 or 90 days and any department: patient visits, average wait, average consultation, urgent cases, confirmed without edits (how often the kiosk history was right), doctor edit rate, average interview, and an estimated time saved (labelled, with minutes per visit set by the administrator). Daily trend, languages, department load and a CSV export protected against spreadsheet formulas. Every view is audited.

Counted one day at a time

The figures come from one row per department and day. The worker recounts today and yesterday every 5 minutes (0.1–0.2 s at a million visits) and every day at 03:07, so a late change to an older visit shows by the next morning. Before 25 October the whole history was recounted every 5 minutes (10 s at a million visits).

Settings

  • Users: create, edit, deactivate; reset a password (changed at first sign-in), unlock, sign out everywhere; two-step sign-in required for administrators.
  • Kiosks: a one-time 24-hour activation code; Online / Offline / Not seen yet; secrets renew every 30 days; phone check-in QRs to print and replace.
  • Departments: code, token prefix (MED → MED-016), names in three languages.
  • Hospital switches: call grace period, Ayurveda, review of systems, online AI, SMS, phone check-in — each audited; risky ones ship off.

Audit log and System

  • Audit log: every sign-in, every view of a case sheet, photo or report, and every change — who, when, which record, never answers or names. Search by record, person, action and dates; export CSV. Nobody can change it.
  • System: version; database, queue store and worker; last backup, disk, certificate; how AI, voice and SMS are set up; each kiosk's last check-in. The same problems reach hospital IT as e-mail alerts.
  • Printables: staff cards, patient signs, the privacy poster and a paper fallback pack.
6 · AI

AI as an assistant, and life without it

AI needs three yeses: the server has a key, the hospital has switched it on, and the patient agreed. Without any of them, every core feature still works on the hospital's server.

FeatureWith AI (Gemini; Sarvam for Indian voices)Without AI, or when the patient says no
English case sheetFree text translated; a short doctor's brief whose every sentence cites its answers, for the doctor to accept, edit or rejectTapped answers translated from the bilingual question bank; free text stays in the patient's words
QuestionsUp to three follow-ups written for this complaint, in three languages, labelled "AI follow-up"The fixed, logical question flow (page 5)

Old papersEach photo read as a draft for the doctor to compareThe photo is kept unread for the doctor to read

Guard-rails on every AI answer

  • An output guard rejects text that diagnoses, advises treatment, names doses or contains links (100 of 100 adversarial prompts blocked in testing).
  • Names and phone numbers are removed before anything is sent; AI red flags must quote what the patient said; AI never lowers urgency.
  • Drafts are labelled until a doctor reviews them; a rejected draft is never printed.
  • If an AI service is slow or down, the kiosk quietly continues with the question bank.

Why this design

A hospital can start with no AI cost and no data leaving the building, and switch AI on only when its contract and its patients allow. Safety never depends on AI being right: rules decide, AI only adds caution, people confirm. A patient who said yes can withdraw from their own phone; from then on nothing more about the visit goes to AI services.

In today's demo check, Gemini (gemini-3.5-flash-lite) answered in 2.4 s and the Sarvam voice in 0.8 s.

7 · Trust

Safety, privacy and security

Clinical safety

  • History only — no diagnosis, disease names, medicines or advice anywhere, in any output.
  • Danger signs first; uncertainty escalates, never lowers urgency; lowering needs a recorded staff decision.
  • The patient confirms the read-back; doctors correct anything; every change is versioned.
  • Red-flag rules, triage weights and wording are drafts with a hazard log; production refuses to start with unsigned clinical content when the hospital requires sign-off.

Privacy (DPDP Act 2023)

  • Consent by purpose: care (required), online AI help and SMS (optional, each with its own notice, withdrawable).
  • A full privacy notice in three languages and a grievance officer.
  • Names, phones, answers, case sheets, notes and photos encrypted in the database; only age and sex required.
  • Status pages show no personal details; a retention tool removes or pseudonymises old records once the hospital approves the periods.

Security

  • Each hospital's rows walled off by the database itself (row-level security), not only by code; least privilege for every service.
  • Two-step sign-in, password reset with forced change, sign-out everywhere; Argon2 password hashes; lockout and rate limits.
  • Kiosks paired with one-time codes; their secrets and the server's keys can be rotated.
  • HTTPS only, strict browser security headers, an append-only audit log with its own screen.
  • On every change: image and code scanners (Trivy, Semgrep, Bandit, Gitleaks, dependency audits); nightly attack scans (OWASP ZAP) and API fuzzing; a threat model and an OWASP ASVS Level 2 self-assessment.

Reliability

  • Every request carries a unique key: a double tap or a retry never creates two visits or two calls.
  • Two people editing one case sheet or one set of notes cannot overwrite each other.
  • If the queue store is lost, the queue is rebuilt from the database; the worker survives a Redis outage.
  • Backups to the minute with a restore drill; 19 tested alerts reach a person by e-mail; failure drills run every night.
For real patients, a penetration test and clinical and legal sign-off still have to happen (page 19). The engineering for them is ready; the decisions belong to people.

Speed and scale

  • Load at a district hospital's peak (300 check-ins an hour, 20 boards, 200 status pages, 8 doctors, 15 minutes): 18,102 requests, 0.02% failed, no token issued twice; 95th percentile check-in step 29 ms, staff board 16 ms, call next 165 ms.
  • A million visits (400,000 patients, 11.5 million answers): the screens staff keep open answer in 9–12 ms after five indexes (migration 0034); query-count tests stop any screen from running one query per row.
  • Reports per day (migration 0035): the 5-minute refresh fell from 10 s to 0.1–0.2 s, with numbers identical to before, row for row.
  • Danger-tile alerts reach staff boards in under a second; a 5,000-patient board applies an urgent change in under 100 ms.
  • Accessibility: no serious or critical issues in automated scans; Lighthouse accessibility 100 on kiosk and status pages; no sideways scrolling from 320 to 1600 px.

11 · For the presentation

Running the demo

Start, check, stop

  1. Start Docker Desktop, then in PowerShell at the repository root: .\start-demo.ps1 -WebPort 8080. The first run builds the images (several minutes). It migrates the database, prepares the demo hospital, 30 days of history and the returning patient, prints the links and accounts, and opens the kiosk.
  2. Check, 30 minutes before: .\scripts\demo-check.ps1 — services, web and API, database, a live AI and voice request, demo patients. It ends with "Ready for the demo".
  3. Phones: .\scripts\lan-check.ps1 when phones on the same Wi-Fi cannot open the QR.
  4. Stop: docker compose down (the data is kept).

A new check-in; on the safety screen tap Chest discomfort. The nurse's board shows an urgent alert at once; after confirmation the patient is first in line, marked "Nurse to confirm".

Do not promise

Real SMS (it is off; patients see the token on screen and on their phone), ABHA/ABDM integration, or any diagnosis. If AI fails, say so at the AI step: every step has a fallback.

12 · Honest status and reference

Limits, what is left, and the words used

Boundaries on purpose

  • No diagnosis, disease names, medicines, doses or drug-interaction checks (ADR 015).
  • It knows only what the patient said: the history is their own account, translated, not verified.
  • Patients see their place in the queue, not their records (a printed PIN is not strong enough sign-in; ADR 014).
  • One server per hospital, inside its network; not built to face the internet.

Before real patients (owned by people, not code)

  • A penetration test by an outside auditor.
  • Clinicians' sign-off of questions, rules and weights; native speakers' review of Hindi and Marathi.
  • Counsel's sign-off of the notice, consent texts, contracts, retention and breach procedure (DPDP Act).
  • A usability test with real staff and patients; real devices over HTTPS on the hospital network; staff training.
  • Waiting on outside parties: an SMS provider and DLT templates, more languages, staging hardware, ABDM.

Words used in the app

TermMeaning
TokenThe patient's number for the day: department prefix and counter (MED-016 = the 16th General Medicine patient today).
OPD cardOPD number (for example 26-000001) and a six-digit PIN on the token slip; links the next visit to this one. The PIN is never stored.
Danger tile, red flagA danger tile is one of six safety buttons on the kiosk; a red flag is a warning raised by a safety rule (or an AI second opinion) from what the patient said. Neither is a diagnosis.
Triage score, head startA 0–100, staff-only score; each point places the patient 0.6 minutes earlier than their arrival, at most 45 minutes. Urgent patients go first.
Grace period, recallThe minutes after a call before staff may mark a patient absent; a recall puts them back on their original arrival time.
AI draft, sourcesThe AI's summary for the doctor, each sentence linked to the answers it rests on; the doctor accepts, edits or rejects it.
Printed rangeThe normal range printed beside a result on the patient's own paper — the only range results are compared with.
Consent purposeCare (required), online AI help and SMS (optional), each with its own notice and record, each withdrawable.
Case sheet, transcriptThe organised English history for the doctor, and the unchangeable record of every question and answer.
Activation code, check-in QRA one-time, 24-hour code that pairs a kiosk; a printed QR that opens phone check-in and can be replaced at any time.
Row-level securityA database feature that makes each hospital's rows invisible to every other hospital.
FHIRThe international standard format for exchanging health records; OPD Saathi exports a visit as a FHIR R4 bundle.
