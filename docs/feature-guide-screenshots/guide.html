The big picture

Government OPDs see hundreds of patients a morning. Patients speak different languages, describe problems in their own way, and wait without knowing their turn. Doctors get a few minutes per patient and spend much of it collecting basic history.

What OPD Saathi does

  • Collects the patient's history before the consultation, in their language, at a kiosk.
  • Spots warning signs and gives the patient a fair place in the queue.
  • Gives the doctor a one-page English case sheet with the patient's own words alongside.
  • Shows staff a live queue and patients a live status page on their phone.
  • Gives administrators reports and control over staff, kiosks and departments.

What it deliberately does not do

  • It never diagnoses, names a disease, suggests medicines or gives treatment advice.
  • It never shows the patient their urgency score — only staff see it.
  • AI can only add caution; safety rules decide, and staff confirm.
  • Clinical questions and rules are drafts until clinicians review them.

2 · Patient kiosk

Check-in, step by step

A touch-screen kiosk (any tablet or PC with Chrome or Edge). Six clear steps are shown at the top of every screen: Agree → Details → Safety check → Questions → Review → Token.

  1. Language. हिन्दी, मराठी or English. The patient can switch language on any later screen too.
  2. Consent. A short notice in plain words, spoken automatically. Nothing is collected before the patient agrees.
  3. Details. Only age and sex are needed. A mobile number is optional and only used, if the hospital turns it on, for SMS queue updates.
  1. Safety check. Chest discomfort, trouble breathing, heavy bleeding, fainting, sudden weakness or difficulty speaking, a seizure happening now. Tapping one sends an urgent alert to staff at once, so a serious patient is noticed within seconds instead of after a full interview.
2 · Patient kiosk (continued)

Smart questions, read-back and token

  1. Questions (8–15). Built from 13 topic modules (fever, breathing, stomach, pain, urine, skin, injury, sleep/energy, past illness, medicines, allergies, family history, anything else). The interview follows the complaint and skips what does not apply — for example, no pregnancy or period questions for men or children. A progress bar shows how many are left. Back changes the previous answer; Stop and exit ends the visit safely at any time.
  2. Review. The whole history is read back in the patient's language. Any answer can be changed before confirming "Yes, this is correct". Confirmation is recorded — doctors can see it.
  3. Token. A large token (for example MED-016), the department to go to, the place in line, and a QR code for the phone. "Print token" prints a slip. The screen resets itself after about a minute for the next patient.
Most answers are a single tap, so even patients who cannot read or type can finish in a few minutes. The patient hears and confirms their own history before it reaches the doctor.
3 · Kiosk comfort, safety and offline mode

Made for every patient

Comfort and accessibility

  • Voice guidance reads every screen aloud; Listen repeats a question. Turn it off with one tap.
  • Speak your answer in Hindi, Marathi or English; the words appear for checking before they are used.
  • High contrast mode for low vision; every button is at least 64 px — easy for older hands.
  • Call staff button on every screen raises a "Patient asked for help" alert in the staff queue.
  • Back and Stop and exit on every step; idle kiosks reset themselves so the next patient never sees someone else's answers.

Safety on the kiosk

  • Danger signs are asked first and alert staff immediately.
  • Answers that describe a warning sign (for example "very high fever", "blood in phlegm") raise a red flag through safety rules — in every language.
  • The patient only ever sees neutral wording; no scores, no disease names.
  • An anonymous "emergency" button was deliberately removed because anyone could trigger false alarms; real emergencies are handled by staff in person.

Old prescription scan AI

On the medicines question the patient can photograph an old prescription; the medicines are read automatically and shown back for the patient to confirm. (Needs the Gemini key.)

Offline short form

If the internet drops, the kiosk offers a short form (complaint, duration, severity, conditions, medicines, allergies, danger signs). It is encrypted on the kiosk, gets a provisional token, and is sent automatically when the connection returns; staff then confirm the real token. Sending twice can never create two visits.

Kiosk setup in one minute

An administrator registers a kiosk in Settings and gets a one-time activation code with a Copy setup link button. Opening that link once on the kiosk device activates it. From then on the kiosk stays signed in by itself — overnight, after a restart, for as long as the administrator keeps it in service — and shows the hospital's name at the top. It reports that it is online about once a minute, so Settings shows it as Online or Offline. If the device is replaced, the administrator issues a new activation code for the same kiosk; its history is kept.

Accessibility is built in, not added on: voice in three languages, high contrast, very large buttons, a call-staff button on every screen, and an offline mode so check-in never stops.
4 · Patient phone status page

"When is my turn?" — answered on the phone

The QR code on the token opens a page on the patient's own phone — no app, no login, no personal details shown.

What it shows

  • The token and the department to go to.
  • Place in line (for example "You are next") and an estimated wait.
  • A four-step progress track: Waiting → Called → With doctor → Done.
  • Language switch (Hindi, Marathi, English).
  • It updates by itself as the queue moves.

Why it matters

Patients can sit down, step outside or look after a child instead of crowding the doctor's door. The link shows only that one visit and contains no name or health details.

Optional SMS updates

If the hospital connects an SMS provider and the patient gives a number and opts in, they receive their token and queue position by SMS (no health details). It is off by default.

The patient leaves the kiosk with a token and a live link — they always know where they stand, which calms the waiting area and reduces crowding at the door.
5 · Staff · Queue

The live queue dashboard

The home page for nurses and doctors: today's waiting room for one department, in fair calling order, updating live.

What is on the page

  • Four counters — Waiting, Called, In consultation, Priority/urgent. Click one to filter.
  • Filters — department, patient status, search by token or complaint, "priority/urgent only".
  • Alerts — red flags, "Patient asked for help" and "AI suggests this patient may be more urgent (please confirm)", each with the reason and the patient's own words, and an Acknowledge button. Optional alert sound.
  • Patients in queue — token and place, age/sex, complaint (translated to English), priority with score and "Why?", waiting time, status.
  • Next to call — who should be called next, with a one-click Call.

Actions

  • Call patient → Start consult → Complete (the main button changes with the patient's state).
  • Open case sheet (document icon).
  • ⋯ menu: mark no-show; change priority (requires a written reason, recorded for audit).
  • Confirm the department when the automatic routing was unsure.

Live and reliable

  • Updates arrive instantly over a live connection; a badge shows Live or Reconnecting.
  • Handles very long queues: a 5,000-patient list applies an urgent change in well under 100 ms.
  • Unfinished visits from earlier days are closed automatically, so every morning starts with a clean queue.
6 · Fair queue

How the triage score and calling order work

The question every OPD asks: "Why was that person called before me?" OPD Saathi answers it with a transparent score that staff can open and read line by line.

The score (0–100) — staff only

  • Built from objective signs: an urgent red flag or danger tile is worth 70, a priority flag 30; warning answers add more (blood in phlegm +25, very little urine +10; at most 25 together); vulnerability adds points (infant under 1 year +20, aged 75+ +12, pregnant +12); a problem that started today +6.
  • The patient's own 0–10 rating is worth at most 4 of 100 points — typing "10" cannot jump the queue.
  • Levels: Routine below 30, Priority 30+, Urgent 70+ or any urgent red flag.
  • Patients never see the score.

The calling order

  • Urgent patients go to the front.
  • Everyone else keeps arrival order, but higher scores get a head start: 0.6 minutes per point, never more than 45 minutes. So a routine patient can only be passed by someone who arrived less than 45 minutes after them — no one waits forever.
  • Staff can override priority, but must type a reason; the change and reason are kept.
  • AI is a checker, not a decider AI: after check-in an AI review may raise an "AI suggests… please confirm" alert; it never lowers urgency, and an AI flag alone counts as priority at most.
  • All weights and red-flag rules live in readable files marked "draft — needs clinician review", so doctors can check every number.
Our queue is fair and explainable: the sickest go first, everyone else keeps their place, a typed "10/10" cannot cheat the system, and any staff member can click "Why?" to see exactly how a position was decided.
7 · Staff · Consultations

The doctor's case sheet

Left: today's patients. Right: one patient, organised into four tabs so the doctor sees what matters first instead of everything at once.

Header (always visible)

  • Token, age, sex, department, language answered in, case-sheet version.
  • Status, priority and score with "Why?".
  • One main action: Call → Start consultation → Finish; plus PDF and a ⋯ menu (print, keyboard shortcuts N, E, P).
  • Green "Confirmed by patient at 10:31" and a separate violet AI notice — patient confirmation never counts as staff checking the AI.

Overview tab

  • Red flags — review required with the reported words, where the flag came from, and "this is not a diagnosis".
  • AI-assisted history summary with "Compare with original answers".
  • Presenting complaint, key history points, and the call → consult → complete workflow from the visit's real status.
7 · Staff · Consultations (continued)

History, transcript and notes

Patient history tab

  • Complaint, history of present illness, past illness, medicines, allergies, family history, tobacco/alcohol, pregnancy.
  • Each field shows its source (voice, tap, typed, nurse, doctor), confidence and an "AI-assisted" tag where relevant, plus the patient's original words.
  • Edit any field. Every save creates a new version; staff edits are marked Staff-verified.
  • If two people edit at once, the second is told and can reload or re-apply their change — nothing is silently overwritten.
  • Leaving with an unsaved edit asks first.

Transcript and notes tabs

  • Interview transcript: every question and the full answer in the patient's language, voice/tap/typed, speech confidence, and "AI follow-up" labels on questions the AI chose. This record cannot be changed.
  • Clinical notes: staff edits on this case sheet, the version and audit note, and corrections the patient made before confirming. (Exam findings and plan are not stored yet — see limitations.)
  • PDF / Print always produce the full case sheet, whichever tab is open.
The doctor gets a one-page English case sheet in seconds — urgent warnings first, an AI brief clearly marked as unverified, and the patient's own Hindi or Marathi words one click away. Every correction is versioned and audited.
8 · Staff · Patients

The patients register

Every visit in a period — not just today's waiting room — for finding a patient, checking what happened, and registering people who need help.

What it shows

  • Status tiles: Checked in, Waiting, With doctor, Completed, Check-in not finished, Cancelled/no-show — click to filter.
  • Filters for period (today, 7 days, 30 days), urgency and department, and a search box.
  • Each visit: token, patient, complaint, urgency, status, language, and a link to the case sheet.

Assisted registration

  • A nurse can start a check-in for a patient who cannot use the kiosk (elderly, unwell, unable to read).
  • Consent is always shown first — staff cannot skip it.
  • The visit then follows the same interview, safety rules and queue as a kiosk check-in.
Nobody is left out: if a patient cannot use the kiosk, a nurse registers them with the same consent, safety checks and fair queue.

10 · Admin · Settings

Administration: users, kiosks, departments

Users

  • Create doctors, nurses and administrators (name, email, initial password of 12+ characters, role, department).
  • Deactivate asks first; the person is signed out within 10 minutes and can be activated again.
  • ⋯ → Delete removes the account for good; their past work stays in patient history and the email can be used again.
  • You can never deactivate or delete yourself.

Kiosks

  • Register → one-time code with Copy code and Copy setup link; valid for 24 hours.
  • Connection: Online, Offline, Not seen yet, Turned off; "Setup code valid until …" while waiting.
  • "Patients registered: N today · M in all" for each kiosk.
  • ⋯ menu: New activation code, Delete.

Departments

  • Code, token prefix (MED → MED-016), and names in English, Hindi and Marathi.
  • Deactivate stops new patients at once.
  • ⋯ → Delete only once no visit is still open there and no staff member is assigned to it.

11 · Admin how-to

Step-by-step administration tasks

Everything below is done by a signed-in administrator in Settings — no commands needed.

Add a department

  1. Departments tab → Create department.
  2. Department code: capital letters, numbers and _ (for example CARDIO).
  3. Queue token prefix: up to 8 capitals/numbers (CAR gives tokens CAR-001, CAR-002…).
  4. Name in English, Hindi and Marathi → Create department.

Automatic routing knows the standard codes (GENMED, ORTHO, ENT, OBG, PAED, DERM, OPHTH, SURG); other departments receive patients sent by staff.

Add a doctor, nurse or administrator

  1. Users tab → Create staff account.
  2. Name, email and an initial password of at least 12 characters.
  3. Role and department (doctors and nurses see their department's queue).
  4. Create staff account, then give the password to the person privately.

Set up a kiosk

  1. Kiosks tab → type a label staff will recognise (OPD entrance, Paediatrics wing) → Register kiosk.
  2. Click Copy setup link.
  3. On the kiosk device, open the link once in Chrome or Edge (press F11 for full screen).
  4. The kiosk shows Start patient check-in and, within a minute, Online in Settings.

For a kiosk on another device, open Settings with the server's network address (e.g. http://192.168.1.20:5173/staff), not localhost, before copying.

Replace a kiosk device, or after clearing its browser

  1. Kiosks → ⋯ on that kiosk → New activation code.
  2. Copy setup link → open it on the (new) device.

Same kiosk, same history. Do not register a new kiosk for this.

Turn something off, or delete it

  • Deactivate = temporary (staff on leave, kiosk under repair, department closed today). Activate brings it back.
  • ⋯ → Delete = permanent (person left, kiosk removed, department closed for good).
  • A department can be deleted only after its open visits are finished and its staff are moved. Unfinished kiosk check-ins close by themselves overnight.

Staff changed department, or forgot their password

There is no edit screen yet. Delete the account and create it again with the same email and the new department or a new password — their past work stays in patient history. If the only administrator is locked out, use create-admin (see Command reference) to add another one.

12 · Scale

One system, many hospitals

Every hospital gets its own OPD Saathi: its own departments, staff, kiosks, patients and queue. Nothing is shared between hospitals, and each hospital's administrator controls only their own.

District Hospital, NagpurOwn server and database12 kiosks → its own department queuesOwn administrator, doctors, nurses
GMC Hospital, PuneOwn server and database30 kiosks → its own department queuesOwn administrator, doctors, nurses
PHC, WardhaOwn server — or shares a district server2 kiosks → its own queuesOwn administrator, doctors, nurses

Recommended: one installation per hospital

  • OPD Saathi runs on a PC or server inside the hospital, with its own PostgreSQL database.
  • Patient data stays in the building, and check-in and the queue keep working if the internet goes down (AI features need internet).
  • Each installation has its own passwords and encryption keys.
  • Installed with one script: .\setup-hospital.ps1 (next section).

Also possible: several hospitals on one server

  • For example a district hosting its PHCs centrally. Add each hospital with .\setup-hospital.ps1 -AddHospital.
  • Staff choose their hospital on the sign-in page; each sees only their own hospital.
  • Separation is enforced by the database itself (row-level security), not just by the app.

How many kiosks?

As many as the hospital needs. Every kiosk belongs to exactly one hospital and sends its patients into that hospital's department queues — 100 kiosks still mean one General Medicine queue, one Orthopaedics queue, and so on, in fair calling order. Tokens are numbered per department per day (MED-001, MED-002…) whichever kiosk the patient used. Each kiosk shows its own hospital's name on screen and on the printed token.

Tested

A second hospital was set up with the new command and checked live: its patient appeared only in its own queue, and neither hospital could open, call, change or delete the other's patients, staff or kiosks (20 of 20 checks passed).

Each hospital gets its own OPD Saathi — its own data, kiosks and staff — and the database itself guarantees that one hospital can never see another's patients.
13 · Installation

Setting up a new hospital

For the person installing OPD Saathi on site. This is separate from the demo — never use start-demo.ps1 for a real hospital.

You need

  • A Windows PC or server on the hospital network that stays on during OPD hours.
  • Docker Desktop installed and running (set it to start with Windows).
  • A copy of the OPD Saathi folder.
  • Kiosk devices (tablet or PC) and staff PCs with Chrome or Edge, on the same network.
  • The hospital's name, a short code for it, and the first administrator's name and email.

What the setup script does

  1. Creates .env with fresh random passwords and encryption keys for this hospital.
  2. Builds and starts the database, cache, API, worker and web app; applies database updates.
  3. Asks for the hospital and its first administrator, then creates them with the 8 standard departments.
  4. Prints the address staff PCs and kiosks use.

Install (PowerShell, in the OPD Saathi folder)

cd C:\OPD-Saathi
Set-ExecutionPolicy -Scope Process -ExecutionPolicy Bypass
.\setup-hospital.ps1

It asks:

Hospital name (shown on its kiosks): District Hospital, Nagpur
Short unique hospital code (e.g. gmc-nagpur): dh-nagpur
Administrator's full name: Dr. A. Sharma
Administrator's email: admin@dhnagpur.example
Administrator password (12+ characters): ************
Type it again: ************

and finishes with:

Hospital ready: District Hospital, Nagpur (019b…)
Administrator: admin@dhnagpur.example
Departments: 8 standard departments created.
  Staff (any PC on the hospital network):  http://192.168.1.20:5173/staff
  Kiosks:                                   http://192.168.1.20:5173/kiosk

Then, the same day

  1. The administrator signs in at the Staff address and opens Settings.
  2. Departments: keep, rename, deactivate or delete the standard ones; add any others.
  3. Users: add every doctor and nurse with their department.
  4. Kiosks: register each kiosk and open its setup link once on that device.
  5. Optional: put GEMINI_API_KEY (and SARVAM_API_KEY) in .env and run .\setup-hospital.ps1 -NoBuild to switch on AI and natural voices.
  6. Take the first backup (see Command reference) and store .env safely with it.

13 · Installation (continued)

Running it day to day

Keeping it running

  • All services restart by themselves when Docker Desktop starts — set Docker Desktop to start with Windows.
  • docker compose down stops everything and keeps the data.
  • Never run docker compose down -v on a hospital server: it deletes the database.
  • Updating: replace the OPD Saathi folder with the new version (or git pull), then run .\setup-hospital.ps1.

Before going live

  • Voice on kiosks needs HTTPS: browsers allow the microphone only on https:// or on the server itself. Until a certificate is set up, network kiosks can tap and type but not speak — or start Chrome on managed kiosks with --unsafely-treat-insecure-origin-as-secure=http://<server-ip>:5173.
  • Back up the database and .env together, regularly.
  • Keep .env private — it holds this hospital's keys.

Updating to a new version

  1. Take a backup (sh scripts/backup.sh) and keep a copy of .env.
  2. Replace the OPD Saathi folder with the new version, keeping the existing .env (or run git pull).
  3. Run .\setup-hospital.ps1. It rebuilds, applies database updates and restarts. Data, staff and kiosks are kept; kiosks stay signed in.
  4. On each kiosk and staff PC, press Ctrl+Shift+R once to load the new version.

A typical morning

  • The server and Docker Desktop are already on; kiosks show Start patient check-in.
  • The administrator glances at Settings → Kiosks: "N of M active kiosks online now". An Offline kiosk usually just needs its device switched on or reconnected to the network.
  • Unfinished visits from the previous day were closed automatically overnight, so every queue starts empty.
Installing OPD Saathi at a new hospital is one script; after that the hospital runs it from the Settings page, and updates keep all data and kiosks in place.
14 · AI

AI features — and life without AI keys

AI makes the system smarter, but it is optional. Without any keys, every core feature still works using rules, the question bank and the browser's own voice.

Guard-rails on every AI answer

  • An output guard rejects any AI text that diagnoses ("you have…"), advises treatment, names doses or contains links.
  • AI red flags must quote what the patient actually said.
  • AI summaries are labelled "not verified by staff" until a person checks them.
  • If an AI service fails or is slow, the kiosk quietly continues with the question bank.

Why this design

Hospitals can start with zero AI cost and zero data leaving the building, then turn AI on when they approve it. Safety never depends on AI being right — rules decide, AI only adds caution, and people confirm.

AI is an assistant, not the authority: it translates, summarises and double-checks, but it can never diagnose, never lower urgency, and the whole system still runs if it is switched off.
15 · Trust

Safety, privacy and security

Clinical safety

  • History only — no diagnosis, disease names, medicines or advice, anywhere.
  • Danger signs first; uncertainty always escalates, never lowers urgency.
  • The patient confirms the read-back; doctors can correct anything; every change is versioned.
  • 15 red-flag rules and the triage weights are readable drafts awaiting clinician sign-off.

Privacy

  • Names, phone numbers, interview answers and case sheets are encrypted in the database.
  • Minimal data: only age and sex are required; phone is optional.
  • The phone status page shows no personal or health details.
  • The offline kiosk form is encrypted on the device and deleted after sync.
  • Optional data-retention tool removes old transcripts and pseudonymises patients.

Security

  • Each hospital's data is walled off inside the database (row-level security), not just in code.
  • Roles: admin, doctor, nurse, kiosk, patient — each sees only what it needs.
  • Passwords hashed; repeated failed sign-ins lock the account and are rate-limited.
  • Kiosks are set up with one-time codes that expire after 24 hours, then sign in with their own device key; deleting or re-pairing a kiosk cuts it off at once.
  • Deactivated or deleted staff lose access within 10 minutes; nobody can lock themselves out.
  • Each installation signs sign-ins with its own private key; the setup scripts never use the published defaults.
  • The web app sends strict browser security headers (content security policy, no framing), so injected scripts cannot run.
  • Every important action goes to an append-only audit log.

Reliability

  • Requests carry a unique key, so a double tap or a retried network call never creates two visits.
  • Two staff editing one case sheet cannot overwrite each other.
  • CSV exports are protected against spreadsheet formula tricks.
  • Automated security checks run on every change (dependency audit, code scanning, secret scanning).
Built for health data from day one: encrypted records, each hospital's data isolated inside the database, role-based access, audit logs, and AI that can never give medical advice.

20 · Help

Troubleshooting

You seeWhy, and what to do
Kiosk shows the activation screenThe kiosk was deleted, set up on another device, or its browser data was cleared. Settings → Kiosks → ⋯ → New activation code → open the setup link on the kiosk.
"The activation code is invalid, expired, or has already been used"Codes work once and for 24 hours. Issue a new activation code.
Setup link opens a blank or wrong page on another deviceIt was copied from a localhost admin page. Open Settings with the server's network address and copy the link again.
Kiosk: "This kiosk is turned off"An administrator deactivated it. Settings → Kiosks → Activate. No new code needed.
Kiosk: "Ask staff to check this kiosk"The hospital has no active department. Activate at least one (normally General Medicine).
Microphone or "Tap and speak" does not work on a network kioskBrowsers allow the microphone only over HTTPS. Set up a certificate, or use the Chrome option in section 13. Tapping and typing still work.
Old version still shows after an updatePress Ctrl+Shift+R (the app keeps an offline copy). Reload once more if needed.
Staff: "account locked"Five wrong passwords lock an account for 15 minutes. Wait, or recreate the account (see Admin how-to).
"Department still in use" when deletingFinish its open visits and move its staff first, or deactivate it instead. Unfinished kiosk check-ins close by themselves overnight.
"Already exists" when creatingThat email, department code or token prefix is already used in this hospital. Choose another, or delete the old record first.
Page will not load / "Cannot connect to the Docker daemon"Start Docker Desktop, wait until it says "Engine running", then run the setup (or demo) script again with -NoBuild.
Port 5173 is in useThe scripts switch to 8080, or choose one with -WebPort.
21 · Honest status

Limitations and next steps

Being clear about what is not done yet makes the presentation stronger, not weaker.

ItemStatus and next step
Clinical reviewQuestion banks, red-flag rules, triage weights and consent text are drafts. They need sign-off by clinicians, and the Hindi and Marathi wording by native speakers.
Doctor's clinical notesExamination findings, assessment and plan are not stored yet; doctors record them in the hospital's own system. Adding them needs a small database and API extension.
Production deploymentA hospital can now be installed with setup-hospital.ps1 (own database, secrets, standard departments). Still to add: HTTPS on the hospital network (needed for kiosk voice), scheduled backups and monitoring.
Settings screensNo screen yet to edit a staff member's details or department, reset a password, switch AI on/off per hospital, or remove a whole hospital. The first three are supported by the server and need small screens; removing a hospital is an operator task.
SMS / WhatsAppThe opt-in and message pipeline exist; a provider account must be connected to actually send messages.
Hospital records integrationNo HIS, ABHA/ABDM or FHIR connection yet — a natural next phase.
Offline voiceThe offline short form works without internet; the full voice interview needs the local server.
Human testingStill to do: run the full demo without internet, and have first-time patients complete check-in without coaching.

Natural roadmap

  1. Clinician and language review of all medical content.
  2. Pilot in one OPD department with real kiosks and staff feedback.
  3. Doctor's notes and e-prescription fields; SMS provider.
  4. ABHA/ABDM linking and hospital-system integration.
  5. More languages (Tamil, Telugu, Bengali…) — the system is built for it.

23 · Glossary

Words used in the app

TermMeaning
TokenThe patient's number for the day, made of the department prefix and a counter (MED-016 = 16th General Medicine patient today).
Danger tileOne of six large buttons on the kiosk safety screen for signs that need immediate attention.
Red flagA warning raised by a safety rule (or a danger tile, or an AI second opinion) from what the patient reported. Not a diagnosis.
Triage score0–100 number, staff-only, that decides how much head start a patient gets in the queue.
Routine / Priority / UrgentScore below 30 / 30 or more / 70 or more or any urgent red flag. Urgent goes to the front.
Head startHow much earlier than their arrival time a patient is placed: 0.6 minutes per score point, at most 45 minutes.
Case sheetThe organised English history the doctor reads, with the patient's original words and the source of every field.
Read-backThe summary the kiosk reads to the patient for confirmation before check-in finishes.
TranscriptThe unchangeable record of every question and the patient's exact answer.
OverrideA staff change of priority or department, which requires a written reason and is recorded.
Assisted registrationA nurse checking in a patient who cannot use the kiosk, with the same consent and safety steps.
Activation codeA one-time secret, valid 24 hours, that turns a browser into a trusted kiosk for one hospital.
Setup linkThe kiosk page address with the activation code attached; opening it once activates the kiosk.
Device keyThe kiosk's own long-lived sign-in, received at activation, that keeps it signed in until it is deleted or re-paired.
Deactivate / DeleteDeactivate turns a record off and can be undone; Delete removes it from Settings for good but keeps all history that refers to it.
Hospital codeA short unique name for a hospital on a server (for example dh-nagpur), chosen at setup.
HeartbeatThe small "I'm on" signal each kiosk sends about once a minute, shown as Online/Offline in Settings.
PWAProgressive Web App — a website that can be installed like an app and keeps working offline for the short form.
Row-level securityA database feature that makes each hospital's rows invisible to every other hospital.

OPD Saathi collects history for clinical staff. It does not diagnose or recommend treatment. All patients, staff and hospitals shown in this guide are fictional.
